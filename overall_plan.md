# Stage 1 and Stage 2 Project Plan

## What Stage 1 establishes

Stage 1 studies individual gene-tree branches.

It asks:

1. Does using an adequate gene-tree model produce more accurate and better-calibrated gene trees?
2. Does model misspecification produce more incorrect branches?
3. Do those incorrect branches receive low or high support?
4. Can support still distinguish reliable branches under model misspecification?
5. Does the relationship between support and branch correctness change as incomplete lineage sorting (ILS) increases?

For every estimated gene tree, branch correctness is evaluated against the corresponding **true gene tree for that locus**, not against the species tree. This distinction is necessary because true gene trees can legitimately differ from the species tree because of ILS. Such disagreement is biological and should not be counted as gene-tree estimation error.

The primary Stage 1 quantity is therefore

\[
P(\text{estimated branch is correct}\mid\text{branch support}).
\]

Stage 1 measures both:

- **gene-tree accuracy**, and
- **whether branch support remains informative about that accuracy**.

However, Stage 1 alone does not show whether these branch-level patterns affect the final species tree. That is the purpose of Stage 2.

---

## Gene-tree model comparison

DNA is generated under:

\[
\text{GTR+G4}.
\]

The same simulated alignments are then analyzed in IQ-TREE under several evolutionary models:

| Analysis model | Role |
|---|---|
| GTR+G4 | Correctly specified reference |
| GTR | Removes among-site rate heterogeneity |
| HKY+G4 | Simpler substitution model while retaining gamma rate heterogeneity |
| HKY | Simpler substitution model and no gamma rate heterogeneity |
| JC | Strongly simplified misspecified model |

This creates a range of gene-tree inference conditions rather than relying only on the extreme GTR+G4-versus-JC comparison.

These models should **not be assumed in advance to form a perfectly monotonic error gradient**. Their actual effect on topology and support must be measured empirically in Stage 1.

IQ-TREE supports JC, HKY, GTR, and gamma-distributed rate heterogeneity through standard substitution-model options, so these conditions do not require hypothetical inference methods.

---

## Varying incomplete lineage sorting

ILS should also be varied because ASTRAL is specifically designed for situations in which true gene trees can disagree with the species tree under the multispecies coalescent.

The experiment should use at least:

- **low ILS**, and
- **high ILS**.

If computational resources allow, a moderate-ILS condition can also be included.

The ILS levels should be generated by changing the species-tree/coalescent simulation parameters in SimPhy and then verified empirically using the disagreement between the **true gene trees** and the **true species tree**.

For example,

\[
d(\text{true gene tree},\text{true species tree})
\]

can be summarized across loci to confirm that the intended low- and high-ILS conditions are actually separated.

This quantity measures biological discordance due to ILS.

It must remain separate from

\[
d(\text{estimated gene tree},\text{true gene tree}),
\]

which measures gene-tree estimation error.

Previous ASTRAL simulation studies have also used SimPhy-based species/gene-tree simulations and varied the amount of ILS through species-tree parameters, providing methodological precedent for this design.

# Stage 1

For every combination of:

```text
ILS level
×
sequence length
×
IQ-TREE analysis model
```

the estimated gene trees are compared with their corresponding true gene trees.

For each estimated internal branch, record:

```text
replicate
locus
ILS condition
sequence length
analysis model
support
correct / incorrect
```

The main Stage 1 analyses are:

### 1. Overall branch error

\[
P(\text{estimated branch is incorrect}).
\]

This determines how much gene-tree estimation error is introduced by each analysis model.

### 2. Support versus correctness

Branches are grouped by support and the project estimates

\[
P(\text{correct}\mid\text{support}).
\]

This determines whether higher support still corresponds to higher reliability.

Each support bin reports its mean support as well as observed correctness, so a
calibration plot compares like with like rather than using only bin endpoints.

### 3. Proper-score calibration summaries

The Brier score is the mean squared difference between branch support and the
0/1 correctness outcome. Lower is better. Expected calibration error (ECE) is
the branch-count-weighted average absolute gap between mean support and
observed correctness within the fixed bins. These give operational numerical
definitions of support reliability in addition to the calibration plot.

### 4. High-support incorrect branches

Important summaries include

\[
P(\text{wrong}\mid\text{support}\ge0.95)
\]

and

\[
P(\text{wrong}\mid\text{support}\ge0.99).
\]

These quantities are particularly important for Stage 2 because wASTRAL gives greater influence to relationships associated with stronger support.

### 5. Effect of ILS

Stage 1 then compares these quantities between low- and high-ILS conditions.

The purpose is to determine whether model misspecification and ILS interact in producing unreliable or confidently incorrect estimated gene-tree branches.

---

## What Stage 1 should establish before Stage 2

The intended Stage 1 comparison is therefore:

```text
analysis model
      +
ILS level
      ↓
gene-tree branch error
      ↓
support associated with correct and incorrect branches
      ↓
reliability of support
```

Possible Stage 1 patterns include:

### Pattern A

```text
GTR+G4:
incorrect branches mostly have low support

misspecified models:
incorrect branches also mostly have low support
```

Support remains informative despite model misspecification.

### Pattern B

```text
GTR+G4:
incorrect branches mostly have low support

increasing model misspecification:
more incorrect branches receive high support
```

Support becomes less reliable as model assumptions are violated.

### Pattern C

The loss of support reliability becomes stronger under high ILS.

This would suggest that difficult biological discordance and model misspecification together create a more challenging input for support-weighted species-tree inference.

These are hypotheses to test, not outcomes that should be assumed in advance.

---

# ASTRAL

ASTRAL combines many gene trees into one estimated species tree.

ASTRAL evaluates quartet relationships induced by the input gene trees and searches for a species tree with high agreement with those gene-tree quartets.

The resolved unweighted analysis ignores support values. A second reference-
aligned baseline contracts branches with aBayes support below 0.90 before
running ASTRAL, representing the uncertainty as polytomies.

For each condition:

```text
estimated gene trees
        ├── resolved ASTRAL
        └── contract aBayes < 0.90 → ASTRAL
```

Both baselines are run for every analysis model and ILS condition.

---

# wASTRAL

wASTRAL is the weighted extension of ASTRAL.

It can use branch-support information to change the contribution of quartet relationships derived from estimated gene trees.

Conceptually:

```text
low-support relationship  → reduced influence
high-support relationship → greater influence
```

wASTRAL does not independently select the highest-support branch from each gene tree. It weights quartet information derived from the gene trees and searches for the species tree with the best total weighted score.

For each model and ILS condition:

```text
estimated gene trees
        ├── support-only wASTRAL mode 2
        └── default hybrid wASTRAL
```

Support-only mode is the primary mechanistic analysis because the project asks
whether branch support remains trustworthy under misspecification. Default
hybrid weighting is included for alignment with how wASTRAL is normally run
and with the headline method in Zhang and Mirarab (2022). Both use `-B` so
aBayes support is explicitly scaled from 1/3 to 1.

---

# Stage 2: weighted versus unweighted comparison

Every gene-tree inference condition therefore produces four species trees:

```text
GTR+G4 gene trees
├── resolved ASTRAL
├── 0.90-contracted ASTRAL
├── support-only wASTRAL
└── hybrid wASTRAL

GTR gene trees
├── resolved/contracted ASTRAL
└── support-only/hybrid wASTRAL

HKY+G4 gene trees
├── resolved/contracted ASTRAL
└── support-only/hybrid wASTRAL

HKY gene trees
├── resolved/contracted ASTRAL
└── support-only/hybrid wASTRAL

JC gene trees
├── resolved/contracted ASTRAL
└── support-only/hybrid wASTRAL
```

This entire comparison is performed separately under low and high ILS.

Because SimPhy supplies the true species tree, every estimated species tree can be compared with the truth.

The primary measurement is species-tree topological error, such as normalized Robinson–Foulds error:

```text
0.00 = estimated species-tree topology is correct
larger value = more disagreement with the true species tree
```

For every analysis-model condition:

\[
\Delta =
\text{weighted error}
-
\text{unweighted error}.
\]

Interpretation:

```text
negative → weighting helped
zero     → weighting made no difference
positive → weighting hurt
```

# Connecting Stage 1 directly to Stage 2

The central project logic is:

```text
gene-tree evolutionary model
          ↓
gene-tree correctness
          ↓
reliability of branch support
          ↓
amount of influence assigned by wASTRAL
          ↓
species-tree accuracy
```

Stage 1 therefore explains **why** Stage 2 might differ between models.

For example:

### Adequate model

If GTR+G4 produces a relationship such as

```text
wrong branch → generally lower support
correct branch → generally higher support
```

then wASTRAL has useful information for reducing the influence of uncertain gene-tree relationships.

Stage 2 may therefore find

\[
E_{\text{wASTRAL}} < E_{\text{ASTRAL}}.
\]

### Misspecified model

If HKY, GTR without gamma heterogeneity, or JC produces more branches that are both

```text
incorrect
+
highly supported
```

then support becomes less effective as an indicator of reliability.

In this case, wASTRAL may no longer gain the same advantage over ASTRAL.

Under sufficiently misleading support, it could potentially perform worse.

### Role of ILS

High ILS can make species-tree inference more difficult because true gene trees contain more biological discordance.

Therefore, systematic gene-tree estimation error may be more consequential when ILS is high.

The project tests whether the Stage 1 support-reliability differences become more strongly reflected in Stage 2 species-tree error under high ILS.

# Experimental comparison

The same simulated DNA is used within every corresponding gene-tree model condition.

| DNA generated with | ILS | IQ-TREE model | Species-tree method | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GTR+G4 | Low / High | GTR+G4 | resolved/contracted ASTRAL | Correct-model unweighted baselines |
| GTR+G4 | Low / High | GTR+G4 | support-only/hybrid wASTRAL | Weighting with correctly specified inference |
| GTR+G4 | Low / High | GTR | resolved/contracted ASTRAL | Misspecification from removing rate heterogeneity |
| GTR+G4 | Low / High | GTR | support-only/hybrid wASTRAL | Test whether weighting remains useful |
| GTR+G4 | Low / High | HKY+G4 | resolved/contracted ASTRAL | Simpler substitution-model baselines |
| GTR+G4 | Low / High | HKY+G4 | support-only/hybrid wASTRAL | Test weighting under substitution-model simplification |
| GTR+G4 | Low / High | HKY | resolved/contracted ASTRAL | Stronger combined simplification |
| GTR+G4 | Low / High | HKY | support-only/hybrid wASTRAL | Test weighting under stronger misspecification |
| GTR+G4 | Low / High | JC | resolved/contracted ASTRAL | Severe misspecified baselines |
| GTR+G4 | Low / High | JC | support-only/hybrid wASTRAL | Stress test when support may be misleading |

Because the same alignments are analyzed under each IQ-TREE model, differences among GTR+G4, GTR, HKY+G4, HKY, and JC are associated with the gene-tree analysis model.

Within each model condition, resolved ASTRAL and both wASTRAL arms start from
the same estimated gene-tree topologies. The contracted baseline deliberately
replaces weak relationships with polytomies. Reporting both baselines separates
the effect of weighting support from the established practice of contraction.

# Main hypotheses

## H1 — Gene-tree model misspecification

Misspecified analysis models will generally increase gene-tree estimation error relative to GTR+G4.

The magnitude and ordering of the effects across GTR, HKY+G4, HKY, and JC will be measured rather than assumed.

## H2 — Support reliability

Under GTR+G4, branch support should remain strongly associated with gene-tree branch correctness.

Increasing model misspecification may weaken this relationship and may increase the fraction of confidently incorrect branches.

## H3 — Species-tree weighting

The benefit of wASTRAL relative to ASTRAL will depend on how informative gene-tree support remains.

Thus,

\[
\Delta =
E_{\text{wASTRAL}}-E_{\text{ASTRAL}}
\]

should become less negative as support becomes less reliable.

Whether it reaches zero or becomes positive is an empirical question.

## H4 — ILS interaction

The consequences of misleading gene-tree support may be stronger under high ILS because competing gene-tree histories already occur more frequently.

This interaction must be tested rather than assumed.

# Contribution

The project should not claim that:

- support weighting itself is new;
- wASTRAL improving ASTRAL is new;
- JC producing poorer gene trees is itself a new finding.

The contribution is more specific:

> **This study tests whether the benefit of support-weighted species-tree inference depends on the evolutionary model used to estimate the input gene trees. It systematically connects model misspecification to gene-tree branch error, support reliability, and the downstream difference between weighted and unweighted ASTRAL, while also testing whether this relationship changes with the level of incomplete lineage sorting.**

The project therefore provides a controlled stress test of an important assumption behind support-weighted species-tree inference:

> **high gene-tree support should correspond to more trustworthy phylogenetic information.**

The contribution is identifying the conditions under which that assumption remains useful and the conditions under which model misspecification weakens it enough to affect final species-tree estimation.

Possible conclusions include:

- **Weighting helps under all tested models:** support-weighted ASTRAL is robust to these levels of model misspecification.
- **Weighting helps strongly under GTR+G4 but progressively less under simpler models:** its benefit depends on gene-tree model adequacy.
- **Weighting stops helping under severe misspecification:** unreliable support removes the advantage of weighting.
- **Weighting hurts under severe misspecification:** confidently incorrect gene-tree relationships can make support weighting harmful.
- **ILS modifies these patterns:** model-induced support problems matter more strongly under greater biological gene-tree discordance.
- **No Stage 2 difference appears despite Stage 1 support differences:** branch-level support degradation does not necessarily propagate to the final species-tree topology.

All of these are scientifically interpretable outcomes.

---

# Final project focus

The project should remain focused on one question:

> **Does gene-tree evolutionary-model misspecification reduce the reliability of branch support enough to change the benefit of support-weighted ASTRAL, and is this effect influenced by ILS?**

Everything in Stage 1 supports this question:

```text
model + ILS
    ↓
gene-tree error
    ↓
support reliability
```

Everything in Stage 2 tests its consequence:

```text
same estimated gene trees
    ↓
resolved/contracted ASTRAL vs support-only/hybrid wASTRAL
    ↓
species-tree error
```

This keeps the project compact while directly linking the gene-tree-level mechanism to the final species-tree result.

# Implemented execution specification

The plan is implemented by `run_project.py` and the class-based modules under
`cse6406/`. The fixed production defaults are:

| Factor | Values |
|---|---|
| Taxa | 51 |
| Loci per replicate | 200 |
| Replicates | 50 independent species-tree replicates |
| Sequence lengths | 200, 800, 1,600 bp |
| ILS treatment | low (`Ne=100,000`), high (`Ne=1,000,000`) |
| Taxon sampling | 50 ingroup + 1 outgroup; one individual per species |
| Species-tree process | birth-only, conditioned on 50 ingroup leaves and height 2,500,000 generations; birth rate `1e-7` |
| Substitution-rate process | exponential with mean `1e-7` substitutions/site/generation (`-su e:10000000`) |
| Generating model | `GTR{1,2,1,1,2}+F{0.30,0.20,0.20,0.30}+G4{0.20}` |
| Analysis models | GTR+G4 correct-model reference; GTR, HKY+G4, HKY, and JC misspecified conditions |
| Unweighted methods | ASTRAL-IV on resolved trees; ASTRAL-IV after contracting aBayes `<0.90` |
| Weighted methods | support-only wASTRAL mode 2 with `-B`; default hybrid wASTRAL with `-B` |
| ILS acceptance | low pooled nRF `<=0.25`; high pooled nRF `>=0.55`; gap `>=0.30` |

### Generating-model parameters in plain language

The generating model is written compactly for AliSim, but each part has a
different biological job:

- `GTR{1,2,1,1,2}` lets different nucleotide changes have different relative
  rates. G-T is the reference rate of 1, so five additional numbers are enough
  to define all six exchangeabilities.
- `+F{0.30,0.20,0.20,0.30}` fixes the expected base composition at 30% A,
  20% C, 20% G, and 30% T. The usefulness of `+F` is that it represents
  unequal nucleotide abundance. A model without this component can be forced
  toward an unrealistic 25%-per-base assumption. In ordinary IQ-TREE
  inference, plain `+F` means frequencies counted from the alignment; in
  AliSim, the values inside braces are the actual frequencies used to generate
  the sequences.
- `+G4{0.20}` divides sites among four gamma-rate categories. Alpha 0.20 gives
  strong among-site rate heterogeneity: many sites change slowly while a
  smaller fraction changes rapidly.

The same exchangeabilities, base frequencies, and gamma shape are used for all
loci. This deliberately removes across-locus substitution-parameter variation
so that analysis-model misspecification is isolated; generalization to
heterogeneous real genomes is a stated limitation.

Population size is the manipulated ILS parameter; the realized treatment is
not assumed. `ils_verification.csv` records true-gene-tree versus true-species-
tree normalized RF for every replicate. Tree histories are generated first,
and the pipeline stops before sequence simulation unless `ils_summary.csv`
meets all three preregistered ILS thresholds in the table above.

Stage 1 missing aBayes labels remain missing in calibration and are counted.
For Stage 2 only, wASTRAL's numeric-label requirement is met by assigning the
documented local-Bayesian lower bound (1/3) to an unlabeled internal branch.
Every imputation is counted in a sidecar audit file. ASTRAL receives the
original topology, so weighted and unweighted analyses still use identical
estimated gene-tree relationships.

The 0.90-aBayes contracted ASTRAL arm follows the comparison used for the S200
dataset by Zhang and Mirarab (2022). Support-only mode remains the mechanistic
test of interest, while default hybrid wASTRAL is included so conclusions can
also address the tool's usual support-plus-branch-length configuration.

Replicate is the independent biological unit. The 50 replicates are treated as
paired blocks across ILS, sequence length, analysis model, and species-tree
method. Branches, loci, and the full grid of condition rows are not independent
replicates. Confirmatory contrasts and multiplicity handling are specified in
`ANALYSIS_PLAN.md`.

The full production grid is intentionally not described as completed. Current
completed results and verification status are recorded in `result.md`.

   https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msac215
